# Your report (ENG-374) and the observed developer profile (ENG-340)

`/stats` is a private report behind the existing Terms gate: a few takeaways, then simple questions about how you code, each with one answer, one small visual and, only where a player needs it, one short line of context.

**Players see insights, not data** (owner direction, 27 October 2026). The page shows no revision hashes, harness or protocol versions, receipt or cache timestamps, formulas, matching or debug statuses, and no methodology disclaimers, not even behind a disclosure. A finding the evidence can't support is left out, not explained. The ENG-340 measurement inventory below (definitions, eligibility, sample rules) is internal documentation and is no longer rendered for players. Consent, privacy choices and the legal notices are separate and unchanged; the report links to the Privacy Notice and settings.

## The report

**A portrait through simple questions** (owner direction, 27 October 2026). `/stats` answers a few questions about how you code, each from evidence already recorded; no new collection. A question the evidence can't answer is left out, never filled with zeroes or explained. There are no inventories (finished, open, revisited, waiting), no chapters and no comparison section. A comparison is at most one tiny FYI on the answer it belongs to. Players see no revision hashes, protocol labels, read times, formulas, matching or debug states, or methodology, even collapsed; the ENG-340 measurement inventory below is internal documentation. Consent, privacy choices and legal notices are unchanged; the page links to the Privacy Notice and settings. `apps/web/lib/report.ts` builds the questions, purely and deterministically (`report.test.ts`).

**Takeaways, then answers** (owner direction, 28 October 2026: derive more meaning from the raw evidence already collected, rather than relabel).
- **One or two solves** are a short first summary ("Your first solve", "Your first two solves"): each answer is one labelled line, no takeaways, and under it the answer's shape when it has one (ENG-394): each solve's submissions in order, two languages as a ring. Never the big typing number, which would only repeat the line.
- **From three solves**, up to three takeaways (`TAKEAWAYS`) lead the report, each a compact count-scoped phrase linking to its answer: recovery after a failed submission, the after-solve confidence answer beside the first result (only when sure and unsure went differently), then first submissions passed.
- **The next challenge** is linked quietly at the top as well as by the button below.
- **Sample size** is named in every answer ("In your one solve", "Across your four solves…"). Answers are observations of those solves, never a habit, trait, skill change or improvement; "typically" marks a median.
- **Pictures** appear only when they add a shape, never to repeat a sentence (ENG-394, owner direction 28 October 2026: tasteful terminal visuals, not one per number). Four kinds, in `report-view.tsx`:
  - **Dotted bars** for amounts set side by side: active editing per solve (earlier and later) and per difficulty, and from three solves how many took 1, 2, 3–4 or 5+ submissions.
  - **Marks** (● passed, ○ didn't), one per item, in order: for one or two solves each solve's submissions up to the solve, shown when one took more than one; for the confidence answer, each answered solve's first submission under what was said. Counts, never a rate. A submission whose result isn't known is **?** ("result not known", named in the legend only when one appears), never a miss: the first submission follows `firstSubmit` (a timeout or an error not classified as the code's is unknown; a compile or runtime error is a known miss), and a later one is a miss only when its checks ran to a verdict on a finished run, since the timeline can't tell a later code error from a timeout.
  - **A ring** only for a real whole split into parts that never overlap: challenges worked on by language (each challenge has one), at most five slices, the rest folded into "N other languages". Never for overlapping clocks such as time to the first edit and active editing.
  - **A split bar** for a two-part whole: challenges inside and outside your listed languages, when both have some.
  Each has a text equivalent: rings and split bars are `role="img"` with every part in words, marks have a screen-reader line per row, and every legend names its parts in text beside the colour. Ring colours are fixed in order (`SLICE_COLOURS`), checked for colour-blind separation on the terminal background, with 2 px gaps between slices.
- **Active editing** is explained once, on the first answer that uses it: time typing, editing or selecting in the editor, with pauses over 30 s left out. It is a different clock from time to the first edit. An isolated paste adds almost nothing, so a pasted solution can honestly read "1 s".

**No model FYI in this version** (a scope decision for ENG-374, not a rule). The earlier FYI set models' submissions beside a person's checks, which aren't the same unit. Submissions against submissions isn't like for like either. Practice lets a person submit only after the current code passes the examples (`session.ts`, the example gate), while a model may submit straight away. The other receipt field that might compare, final code length, has no counterpart in the code-free attempt summaries. So the report shows no FYI and requests no model results; practice's completion screen keeps its own. A compact matched model observation is still welcome when one is genuinely like for like. Human comparisons stay off (`COHORTS_ENABLED = false`, [human cohorts](human-cohorts.md), ENG-376); when enabled, they would be a single FYI on the relevant answer, in the same units.

**Still to discover** (owner direction, 28 October 2026). At the bottom of the report (and under the empty report's start) is a short list of every supported question this player's practice can't answer yet. Each entry gives what would answer it, as the real requirement, and one specific step where one exists:
- **Before any solve:** one line names whichever of start, iterate and languages aren't answered yet, since the first solve answers them. Languages is left out when practice in progress already answers it.
- **Difficulty:** a challenge at another difficulty in the latest current-version solve's language, named and linked.
- **Later vs earlier:** another challenge at the latest solve's language and difficulty, named and linked (practice's `nextProblem`, restricted to that language and level).
- **Recovery:** shows once a submission doesn't pass and a better one follows before the challenge is solved. There's no link, since nobody should fail on purpose.
- **Confidence:** shows after two answers (or one more) to "how sure were you?", described as an optional question offered now and then after a solve. Practice offers it on a rotating schedule (`solve-reflection.ts`), so the list never promises it after the next solve.
- **Typing:** about 50 hand-typed code characters. With Vim keybindings on, it says the rate isn't measured, rather than asking for more practice.
- **Repeat:** solve a challenge again in the same setup, linked to the earliest solve on the challenge's *current* version. A repeat compares one version, so an older version can't be the baseline.

There are no unlock counts beyond the real ones. Familiarity isn't listed: the languages invitation already offers it, and a hidden invitation stays hidden. **Comparison lines** (ENG-394 bridge over ENG-376's contract, [comparison FYI](comparison-fyi.md)). ENG-376's helpers decide what may be said (`humanFyi`, `modelFyi`, their candidates, thresholds and reasons); Stats only wires them (`app/stats/comparisons.ts`). `useReportData` reads cohort summaries and model receipts per exact challenge revision, stored under `challenge/revision` so each read is bound to the pair that asked, and this device's passing-code lengths as numbers from `PracticeStore.solvedCodeLengths` (no code leaves the store). All of it is cleared at every boundary and late replies are discarded, and the personal answers never wait on it. At most one line about other players and one about AI models appear after the answers, each the helper's own sentence naming its challenge, never beside a figure across several solves. A line that isn't shown leaves one note with its real reason under Still to discover (adults only with the optional age step; solved challenges on current versions in one known setup; not available right now; at least `MIN_COHORT` comparable adults, with the helper's neutral settings-review link, since a local age answer alone isn't being counted; no matching model setup), nothing while loading, and nothing for 16–17. Off by default: human lines follow `COHORTS_ENABLED`, model lines `MODEL_FYI_ENABLED` (false), and a line that's off requests nothing. A development build can preview both against synthetic replies with `NEXT_PUBLIC_STATS_COMPARISON_PREVIEW=1` (`tools/browser/stats-comparisons-check.ts`); production builds compile that away.

**Share a summary** (ENG-394). A quiet "Share a summary" beside the next challenge opens a preview of the exact plain text it offers, and the player chooses to copy it, use the system share sheet (where the browser has one) or download it as a text file; closing the preview does nothing. The text is a few counts (`ShareSummary`, `shareText` in `report.ts`): challenges solved, by catalog language when every solve has one, and how many first submissions passed, with a link to the site. That last line keeps the iterate answer's scope: when some solves' first result isn't known it says "in 1 of 1 solve with a known result", never a bare "passed". Never challenge names, times or dates, code, profile or after-solve answers, or any identifier. Nothing is posted, uploaded or logged, and no link carries the counts. It isn't offered before a solve. The preview always shows the current report, so a change in practice updates it; stealth, logout, reset, deletion and import remove the report, and the preview and its dialog with it. Copy falls back to select-and-copy, and says so when that fails too. Copying and sharing finish after the click, so each belongs to an operation that ends on unmount, a change in the text, closing or reopening the preview, or the next action: a Clipboard write refused after its operation ended never falls back to select-and-copy, and no late Copied or failure shows (`copyText`'s `current` check; result sharing keeps the default). A write the browser already accepted can't be taken back. On short screens the dialog widens so its actions stay in view; the preview scrolls.

**Next challenge.** An unfinished attempt comes first. Otherwise the report offers what practice would after the latest thing worked on (`nextProblem`): the same language, then the player's listed languages, then the nearest difficulty, so two JavaScript solves lead to the next JavaScript challenge, not the first in catalog order.

**Caching, precisely.** Two short-lived caches exist, both from existing infrastructure:
- uploaded-history summaries are kept in page memory, keyed by participant, while the report is open and for at most five minutes after (a timer drops them);
- model receipt metadata is cached on the server for 60 seconds per Worker isolate (at most 32 revisions), for practice's completion screen; the report no longer requests it.

There is **no** scheduled or durable global precomputation, and **no** new retained personal-summary cache: nothing about a player is stored for the report beyond what practice already keeps. A prefilled, clock-refreshed global bundle would need new scheduling or configuration and isn't built.

A new player sees one line and a start. Someone who has started but has nothing answerable yet sees one line and a resume. Stealth shows only what stealth means and a link to privacy settings. The page uses the shared terminal geometry.

### Data, freshness and boundaries

On an ordinary visit the report reads this device's evidence first and renders it. In parallel it reads the owner's uploaded summaries (the past 366 days, first page of five attempts, through the existing secret-verified `/api/private-history`). It requests no model results (see "No model comparison" above). Uploads arrive independently; a slow or failed read shows a one-line status, never a blocked report. Uploaded summaries stay in page memory while the report is open and, keyed by participant, for at most five minutes after (a timer drops them), so moving around the site doesn't re-query. The report reads the first page automatically; there is no player-facing paging. A rate-limited or failed read shows only that earlier practice couldn't be added. Boundaries are counted per tab, so a nested one (deletion wraps reset) keeps every report blocked until the outermost finishes, including a report opened mid-boundary.

Device and uploaded attempts are deduplicated by attempt and revision (the trace with the later last observation wins, the device on a tie). Chapter 1 labels whether uploads contributed. Nothing read from uploads is written to progress, storage or backups.

Reset, deletion and import start a private boundary: the report, pending reads and the in-memory copy are cleared synchronously and reads are blocked until the boundary finishes. The open report then re-reads only this device and shows "uploads not re-read"; it never re-reads uploads in that same interaction, and nothing is ever written back to progress. A later deliberate visit (a new mount) reads still-retained uploads normally, because reset doesn't delete them (the reset page and the Privacy Notice say so). Account deletion clears the browser's identity, so no later visit can request history either ("no uploads linked"), and the server's tombstone refuses any request regardless. Logout, consent changes and stealth clear everything and read nothing. A boundary while `/stats` is closed still drops the in-memory copy (listeners are registered once per tab).

**Stealth** means no report: no device read, no history request, no receipt request. Server-side, stealth is a browser setting the server cannot read without another owner-scoped query, so the private-history route does not enforce it; this is not a privacy gap, since that route returns only the verified owner's own summaries, and the receipt route returns no personal data. Exclusion of stealth participants from any future human aggregate must be enforced where aggregates are computed, using the uploaded stealth answer (`site.profile`), not by hiding UI.

## Evidence and definitions

`packages/developer-profile/registry.ts` is the definition and threshold registry. `developer-profile-2` is an interpretation version, independent of capture. Every metric needs at least one eligible attempt/revision. There is no threshold to unlock a rank or percentile. Counts are per attempt; the overview of a metric is the median across eligible attempts, except first-submit success, which shows numerator/denominator. These medians describe the selected task mix; they do not establish growth. Repeats can be filtered separately; without an explicit repeat start or an earlier retained completion, first/repeat status remains unknown.

The pure reader deduplicates on stored `recordId` (the server's `record_id`), then orders each source by sequence. It uses the original per-key timestamps, never the batch delivery time. Wall clock orders separate sources approximately; it never subtracts independent monotonic clocks. Missing positions, explicit capture gaps, conflicting IDs, non-monotonic clocks, unsupported capture versions and missing starts withhold derived values for that attempt. Missing values are `null`, never fabricated zeroes. No retained trace can establish that an unseen closing tail was recorded.

## Local history and privacy

**Release gate:** `PROFILE_HISTORY_ENABLED = false`. The current Privacy Notice promises latest-attempt-only local storage; ENG-340 does not change consent or that promise. By default the dashboard uses the latest raw attempts retained on this device; explicit uploaded-history loading is separate. The tested summary path is implemented for a subsequent approved disclosure/version change, but the uploader does not enable it.

When enabled, before compaction removes an older uploaded attempt, the existing IndexedDB transaction saves its code-free interpretation in `settings[developer-profile-summaries]`. An attempt with any queued observation stays whole until it can be summarized atomically. The latest attempt's raw evidence remains available as before. If summary derivation/storage fails, the transaction aborts without deleting raw evidence. Summaries include the interpretation version, private attempt identity/revision, counts, conditions, optional retrospective answers and at most 200 timeline labels. They contain no code, raw keys, test outputs or reference solutions and are never uploaded.

The reader combines these summaries with current local records, with current records taking precedence. History compacted before this feature is not recoverable here. Changing interpretation versions invalidates old summaries; absent raw evidence is not silently reinterpreted. This disabled local alternative adds no public ID-based reader. The approved option A implementation below reuses existing CF queries and erasure, with the separately approved verification binding.

Existing backup export/import carries summaries in the store settings. Reset clears them with observations while keeping the device proof so uploaded data stays deletable. Account deletion clears the identity and summaries too. Reset change notifications occur after the clear, so open dashboards cannot refresh before deletion finishes. No server derived store or public response is added, so existing server erasure needs no new propagation target.

Stealth keeps own private stats available, hides all human/model comparison details and makes no comparison requests. Future aggregate release still must exclude stealth participants, including historical data, enforce approved minimum counts across combined slices, and keep 16–17-year-olds separate or excluded. None of that is bypassed by this dashboard.

No event, question, runtime tooling or model call is added. The draft Privacy Notice/version changes for eventual release; no live consent change is made. Local historical retention remains off under option A. Profile context uses existing editable/skippable settings, coordinated with ENG-333/356–367 by linking rather than creating a new prompt mechanism. No age nudge, demographics gate or promised percentile.

## Root-review corrections and model receipt boundary

Interpretation v2 adds genuine pre-test user-code error recovery. `errored` alone is not a classifier: it must join a terminal `user-error` with compile/runtime phase; runner/loading failures, cancellations, timeouts and unclassified errors remain separate. Reaching tests after such an error is an observed improvement, not completion. First-submit success treats that genuine user failure as unsuccessful and keeps infrastructure/unclassified outcomes unavailable.

Private snapshots now use a generation-checked external store. Reset/delete/import start a transient same-tab/BroadcastChannel invalidation before asynchronous storage work; privacy/account/progress storage events also invalidate. Event handlers flush the cleared view synchronously, pending generations cannot publish success/error/loading over the boundary, and clearing blocks rereads until it finishes. Failed post-boundary reads have no old snapshot to retain. Ordinary focus/manual refresh failures may retain a clearly labelled stale view. Logout/invalid consent prevent reads. Model requests have their own generation/abort boundary and cannot restore results after stealth/reset/logout. This adds no observation or persistent history.

`GET /api/model-receipts/<key>/<revision>` returns only allowlisted offline model metadata for a registered public revision behind the current Terms gate. It accepts no participant ID, code, profile or history. It uses existing catalog D1 and private CONTENT R2 bindings, querying fixed slot fields for the current harness and reading fixed `model-runs/stored/` then `outbox/` keys. The server validates model actor/session/revision/source coverage, ledger/start/end agreement, exact system/tool protocol and reasoning configuration, completion checks, runtime and feature allowlists before projection. No code, output, prompts, reasoning, arbitrary error/reason strings, artifacts, run IDs or participant IDs are returned or logged. No benchmark execution or credentials are added.

Reads cap at 25 slots and 8 MiB per receipt with a real 15-second database/stream deadline, with truncation/read-unavailable states instead of made-up outcomes. Server metadata cache is bounded to 32 revision keys for 60 seconds, coalesces duplicate revision reads, and allows at most two uncached reads per isolate; HTTP responses use no-store. Missing objects are not model failures. Existing R2 copies already used by the model event store are reused; no derived store or player-erasure target is added. Model results refresh separately from immediate local completion. ## Loading repair

Existing device progress appears before IndexedDB interpretation. The selective evidence reader excludes code artifacts, upload queue and unrelated settings. Local storage and identity waits have eight-second deadlines. Model reads have a twelve-second end-to-end deadline and at most two concurrent requests; transient capacity results get at most one retry after the batch. **Pre-actor uploads (`identity-actor`).** The reader checks every selected observation's actor. Practice captures 1.0.0 and 1.1.0 (before `4a263e3`) had no `actor` key. The client envelope always wrote schema 1.0.0 and a dated Terms version; ingest checked none of the three. Some such records were uploaded, and one of them refuses the whole read as `identity-actor`, while `derive` skips the same records on the device.

This is a reproduced, source-supported explanation of the diagnostic; the owner's records have not been examined. Both readers now use `practiceActor`:
- **An explicit actor on a version other than 1.0.0 and 1.1.0** is read as it says (`person` or `model`).
- **An actorless record** is a person's only in the exact pre-actor form: schema 1.0.0, capture 1.0.0 or 1.1.0, and a dated Terms version.
- **Still refusing the read:**
  - a model;
  - a malformed or null actor;
  - an actorless record of any other version or schema, including current 1.2.0;
  - a 1.0.0 or 1.1.0 record that names an actor;
  - a missing or undated Terms version.

Ownership, attempt, observation, challenge and revision checks are unchanged. Versions `derive` doesn't support, 1.0.0 among them, are still flagged "Unsupported capture version."

**History recovery.** A failed uploaded-history read keeps everything already shown and records a fixed reason code: `http-<status>`, `http-<status>:<server diagnostic stage>`, `timeout`, `network`, `identity` or `invalid-page`. The code goes on the status line's `data-history-reason` attribute and into one `console.info` line, never into the visible copy, and never includes the request, identity or response text. Rate limits, 5xx, timeouts, network errors and a slow identity read are retried automatically up to twice: identity after 10 s, everything else at the server's 61 s read interval. A body that is slow or cut off counts as a timeout or network failure; only a complete body that isn't a page object is `invalid-page`. Ownership refusals (401/403/410), other 4xx and malformed pages are not retried. After that, a "Try again" button and returning to the tab retry once more. One pacer per tab (`HistoryPacer`) holds the 61 s read interval and the retry budget across report remounts: a remount, click or focus inside the interval waits for it ("Adding your earlier practice…"), and only a successful page refills the budget. Reset, deletion, import, logout and stealth cancel pending retries and discard late replies as before.
