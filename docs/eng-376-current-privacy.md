## Publication contract and authority

The requester approved refining the **authorization-point** contract in draft code: after a server-acknowledged privacy/deletion mutation, a newly authorized comparison cannot use the old generation. An earlier-authorized response may arrive later. This is not network-delivery retraction. [The exact RPC/control proposal](eng-376-publication-contract.md) specifies signatures, fields, configuration, recovery and cache integration. All live authority/activation remains approval-gated.

Implemented in this disabled draft:

- Original-secret-authorized `POST /ingest/v1/participants/<id>/comparison-privacy`, action `read` or `update`; conditional `expectedRevision` protects stale tabs. Existing ownership proofs and participant/address rate limits are reused; missing proof is not an enrollment claim. No ID-only public lookup.
- CONTROL `comparison-privacy/<id>` = `{stealth, age, revision, acknowledged}`. Age is exactly `adult`, `non-adult`, or `unknown`; unanswered is never adult. A provisional write is ineligible and readable only as pending until generation invalidation and conditional finalization succeed. Telemetry cannot update it.
- CONTROL `comparison-publication/fence` = `{generation, authorizationNonce}`, both server-generated UUIDs. Every restrictive **and permissive** change advances generation before success. Both authorization and invalidation use conditional ETags, fresh rereads after conflicts, at most three attempts, and fail closed. Authorization never writes an old generation over a concurrent invalidation.
- Separate internal `ComparisonEligibility.check(members)`, `generation()`, `authorize(generation)` entrypoint, disabled without the ingest flag and lacking a live binding. `ParticipantOwner.verify` is unchanged. Member IDs never reach public output; no arbitrary SQL, key, raw event, secret or proof access.
- When the ingest flag is eventually approved/enabled, deletion persists its tombstone and advances generation before acknowledging success. The ownership proof stays until invalidation succeeds, so partial deletion is retryable with the original secret. Tombstones exclude immediately from current member checks; physical erasure is unchanged. Privacy state is removed on completed deletion. A privacy write already in flight may leave redundant provisional state, but its tombstone still wins.

No date, counter or client record ID establishes privacy order: the conditional fence write is the authority. At most three CAS attempts is a contention bound, not a guarantee that every requested write succeeds. Unacknowledged failures remain visibly pending and must not claim “nothing was deleted.”

## Settings and stale tabs

Privacy and age settings now mount the disabled sync controller. Local changes take effect immediately. The view distinguishes disabled, pending/offline, confirmed, conflict and deleted state. Acknowledgements and revisions are memory-only, scoped to browser identity plus the exact current preference. Refresh/focus/online reads never silently enroll a browser or re-enable comparisons.

Saves use only a previously observed revision (or create-only null). A conflict reads the current server state for explicit review; it never reads and blindly overwrites. An explicit retry can recover a lost success response or a provisional state. A provisional state matching the preference stays pending; a different provisional state requires review. Reload does not trust a persisted confirmation. Reset/delete/import/logout and cross-tab changes invalidate obsolete reads/writes; late responses cannot restore a previous confirmation. The privacy heading follows current cross-tab local settings. Unknown/minor eligibility never becomes adult.

The future server acknowledgement governs **human comparison publication**, not data capture/upload or internal statistics. Current stealth still hides the whole portrait and comparison requests. No new historical retention is added. Settings copy explicitly distinguishes local changes from confirmation and acknowledges that earlier-authorized/downloaded aggregates cannot be recalled. The Privacy Notice/version still requires review before activation; this task does not silently change consent.

## Fixed query and measured denominators

Each exact candidate query is for a registered current challenge/test revision. Its server-selected interval begins at the later of immutable registry publication time or 90 days before computation, and ends at computation time; both `received_at` and `occurred_at` are bounded. No caller date, SQL, browser ID or demographic slice is accepted by the public route. Delayed pre-window events cannot join a partial attempt at the window edge.

First means **first observed on this revision since its registry publication**, not verified lifetime-first coding experience. First groups require the window to include publication and a unique earliest observed start, including starts belonging to otherwise excluded attempts. In a truncated window, ambiguous firsts are withheld. Repeat requires explicit `again` or an earlier retained completion. One browser contributes at most one selected first and one selected repeat; resets/missing history do not prove never having tried a task. Ingest/client clocks and delayed capture still limit any observational first/repeat inference. Native Basin validation of the exact query remains separately coordinated.

Each attempt needs one known start, one exact runtime and editor/feature condition across resumes, supported capture, current Terms-bearing person records, no conflicting copies, and contiguous per-source positions. Explicit capture gaps exclude it. Profiles with newer conflicting copies fail closed rather than resurrecting an earlier adult/non-stealth answer. Uploaded profiles remain only a conservative extra filter; current acknowledged CONTROL state and tombstones are authoritative.

Exact editor features separate PR75 `mod-enter-runs-examples` from older submit shortcuts, standard/vim keys, diagnostics and hover/tool conditions. Unknown features withhold a group. Runtime projection must match the server's pinned current language runtime; uploaded arbitrary runtime fields cannot escape in an artifact/FYI. This initially withholds older runtime versions. No claim about unobserved external assistance is made.

- Observed completion share: validated started browser attempts, including observed unfinished/unsubmitted starts (right-censored evidence, not a stable success probability). Completion requires a matching successful submitted run and completion record.
- First-try share: only first submissions with an actual consistent decided test verdict, positive integer total, valid pass count and rule array. Errored/cancelled/unknown first verdicts are unavailable for this metric, not failures or zeroes. This deliberately describes first **decided-test** outcomes; it is not the all-starters first-submit success rate.
- Submissions-to-solve distribution: distinct run IDs only, up to the matching first solve, among finishers with valid submission evidence. Post-solve submissions do not inflate it. Ambiguous cross-source same-time ordering or conflicting/missing run IDs withhold the attempt.

Each metric independently needs 20 eligible distinct browser participants. `denominators` supplies bands for completion, first-try and finisher subsets; a suppressed metric has null denominator/value. Browser IDs are not verified people. Five-percent rounding and thresholding reduce disclosure precision but do not guarantee anonymity or prevent boundary inference.

## Private hourly handoff, no request-time global scan

The implemented `cohortDiscoveryQuery(current)` / `selectHourlyCohorts(rows,current,now)` seam avoids a blind per-revision hourly fan-out. One fixed batched discovery scan screens at most 100 trusted current revisions for 20 distinct potential starters within the same maximum 90-day windows; it returns revision pairs only. Exact current eligibility and per-metric thresholds are still required. The proposed scheduler launches at most 12 exact builds after that discovery: at most **13 human Basin queries per invocation**, with two active exact builds. A stable 69-candidate set rotates over six hourly invocations; one-hour snapshot expiry still applies, so this capacity limit can omit FYIs. No scheduler, cursor store or cache persistence is added here. Full bounds, failure semantics and integration ownership are in the publication contract.

`buildHumanSnapshot(spec, query, control)` and `serveHumanSnapshot(raw, spec, control)` in `packages/statistics/human-snapshot.ts` are pure interfaces for the separately owned private scheduled cache. The spec binds challenge/revision, a validated window for builds, and the trusted pinned runtime for both build and serve. The artifact is `{version, challenge, revision, window, computedAt, expiresAt, generation, groups}`: aggregates only, no member IDs. First generation is captured before the fixed query/current checks, and a change during construction rejects the artifact.

An hourly TTL is explicit and bounded to one hour. Serving projects allowlisted aggregate fields and calls conditional `authorize(generation)`; missing/expired/malformed/mismatched/invalidated data omits the FYI. No stale fallback or SQL on page loads. The public `cohort()` seam remains unavailable even if the source read flag is prematurely flipped, until the separately reviewed snapshot reader is installed. `precomputeCohort()` is the disabled internal builder seam; no scheduler calls it here.

Any acknowledged privacy mutation invalidates all old-generation artifacts immediately for new authorization, independent of the hourly cadence. Member lists are never cached as eligibility authority. Cache persistence, triggers, precomputation cadence/cost review and model summaries remain with the requesting owner. Personal local answers and tiny comparison FYIs keep their existing presentation.

## Bounds

SQL output is capped at 1,000 member rows (1,001 overflow sentinel), 1 MiB streamed bytes and 15 seconds for fetch/body. At most 100 unique browser IDs are checked, without privacy/tombstone caching; two passes cost at most 600 CONTROL reads. Final serving uses only artifact projection plus bounded conditional generation authorization, not member scans or SQL. The internal builder retains two concurrent queries and one query per revision per minute per isolate, with at most 128 recent keys. These are **not** global spending caps.
